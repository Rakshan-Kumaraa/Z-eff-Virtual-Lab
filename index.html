Aim, Physical Principle & Slater’s Empirical Rules

Understand how inner-shell and same-shell electrons screen nuclear attraction and govern atomic radius, ionization enthalpy, and electronegativity.

1. Aim & Fundamental Principle Core Concept

In a multi-electron atom, an outer electron does not experience the full Coulombic attraction of the positively charged nucleus (Z protons). Inner core electrons and other electrons in the same shell repel the target electron, creating a negative screening cloud that reduces the nuclear pull.

Zeff = Z − σ
  • Z (Atomic Number): Total positive nuclear charge (number of protons).
  • σ (Screening / Shielding Constant): Dimensionless measure of net inter-electronic repulsion computed via Slater’s empirical rules (1930).
  • Zeff (Effective Nuclear Charge): Net positive charge felt by a target electron in a specific subshell.
Orbital Penetration Order: For a fixed principal quantum number n, radial probability density near the nucleus decreases in the order ns > np > nd > nf. Hence, s and p orbitals shield more strongly and experience greater nuclear attraction than d and f orbitals.
2. Slater’s Grouping & Shielding Multipliers Algorithm

Step A — Arrange Subshells into Slater Groups (ordered from inner to outer shells, grouping ns and np together while keeping nd and nf separate):

(1s) (2s, 2p) (3s, 3p) (3d) (4s, 4p) (4d) (4f) (5s, 5p) …

Step B — Apply Shielding Weights (σi) per Electron:

Group Position Relative to Target Target in (ns, np) Target in (nd / nf)
Groups to the right (outer) 0.00 0.00
Same group (−1 target e⁻) 0.35 (0.30 for 1s) 0.35
Groups in (n − 1) shell 0.85 1.00
Groups in ≤(n − 2) shells 1.00 1.00
Worked Example A: p-Block — Nitrogen (Z = 7) 2p Valence Electron

Ground State: 1s² 2s² 2p³ → Slater Groups: (1s)² (2s, 2p)⁵

  • Same group (2s, 2p): 5 − 1 = 4 other electrons × 0.35 = 1.40
  • (n − 1) shell (1s): 2 electrons × 0.85 = 1.70
  • Total Shielding (σ): 1.40 + 1.70 = 3.10
  • Effective Nuclear Charge (Zeff): 7 − 3.10 = 3.90
Worked Example B: d-Block — Zinc (Z = 30) 4s vs. 3d Comparison

Slater Groups: (1s)² (2s,2p)⁸ (3s,3p)⁸ (3d)¹⁰ (4s,4p)²

  • For 4s valence electron: σ = (1 × 0.35) + (18 × 0.85) + (10 × 1.00) = 25.65 → Zeff(4s) = 30 − 25.65 = 4.35
  • For 3d penultimate electron: (4s)² lies to the right (0.00); σ = (9 × 0.35) + (18 × 1.00) = 21.15 → Zeff(3d) = 30 − 21.15 = 8.85

Atomic Range:
p-Block Period SlopeΔZeff = +0.65

Across Period 2 (Li to Ne) or Period 3 (Na to Ar), each added electron enters the same (ns, np) group and shields by only 0.35. Thus Zeff rises by 1 − 0.35 = +0.65 per proton, shrinking atomic radii across the period.

3d Transition Series SlopeΔZeff(4s) = +0.15

From Sc (Z=21) to Zn (Z=30), differentiating electrons populate the inner (n − 1)d = 3d orbital, shielding the outer 4s electron by 0.85. Consequently, Zeff(4s) increases by only 1 − 0.85 = +0.15 per proton.

Alkali Metal Resetn → n + 1

Stepping from a Noble Gas (Ne, Zeff = 5.85) to the next Alkali Metal (Na, Zeff = 2.20) places the new electron into a fresh principal shell where the previous valence shell becomes an (n−1) core shield (0.85 weight).
